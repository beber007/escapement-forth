Escapement Forth

Escapement's Forth console and its real-time kernel, compiled to WebAssembly. They run here, in this page. Follow the tour or type your own words: the timeline and the pool show what the kernel does.

  • hard real time · EDF
  • 4 tasks · 1, 10, 100, 1000 ms
  • the board's code · unchanged
  • in your browser · nothing is sent
  1. Four tasks, sixteen slotsThe kernel has four tasks, with periods of 1, 10, 100 and 1000 ms. Each has four slots. You attach words to them from the console: ' blink 100 every.
  2. On trial firstA new word is on trial. It runs every period and the kernel measures its time. It gets a tenth of its period. If it goes over, it gets cut.
  3. Promoted or refusedAfter ten measures within its budget, promote guarantees the word. One condition: all promoted words must fit in 70 % of the processor.
  4. Deadline firstThe kernel always runs the task whose deadline is closest (EDF). The 1 ms task interrupts the 1 s task. The console comes after all of them.

What this page is, and what it is not

    
    
      
  • The Forth and the kernel are the board's code, compiled as is: the console and
        the slots (src/), Escapement's hard kernel under EDF (EscapementHard.c) and
        the Pico's four tasks, one per period. Only the layer below changes (web/platform_web.c,
        web/kernel/).
    • 
      
  • Here, the console is the kernel's background task. The slots interrupt it, and each
        other, where the Forth reads the clock: every sixteen words. On the board, the console has its own core and never delays a slot.
    • 
      
  • The kernel's clock follows the browser's. The browser only runs the page every few
        milliseconds, less often in a hidden tab. The kernel then catches up, release by release:
        each on time for the kernel, late in real time. Beyond 200 ms, the rest is dropped. A
        browser pause of more than about half a millisecond in the middle of a run (garbage
        collection, the system) is left out of the clock and of the measures. The board has no
        such pauses.
    • 
      
  • The times shown are your browser's, not the Pico's. They are read to a tenth of a
        millisecond, or to a millisecond depending on the browser, and more coarsely where it
        protects your privacy, which the page then says. A very short word can show 0, and an
        empty task has no width on the timeline. This page says nothing about what the kernel
        costs on a real chip, nor about its energy.
    • 
      
  • A typed line is stopped after 10 s, like Ctrl-C on the board. The dictionary lasts
        as long as the page: save has no flash to write to.
