Escapement Forth

The Forth console of Escapement, and the real-time kernel under it, compiled to WebAssembly and running in this page. Follow the guided tour, or type your own words; the timeline and the pool below show what the kernel does with them.

  • hard kernel · EDF
  • 4 tasks · 1, 10, 100, 1000 ms
  • the board's code · unchanged
  • in your browser · nothing sent
  1. Four tasks, sixteen slotsThe kernel runs a task every 1, 10, 100 and 1000 ms. Each holds four slots, to which the console binds words: ' blink 100 every.
  2. A trial, with a budgetA word bound is on trial: it runs at each release and its time is measured. A run may take a tenth of its period; past that budget it is cut.
  3. Promoted, or refusedpromote admits a word measured ten times within its budget, with a worst case drawn from them, while the promoted words fit in 70 % of the processor.
  4. Deadline firstThe kernel runs the task whose deadline comes first, EDF: the task of 1 ms interrupts that of 1 s, and the console waits for them all.

What this page is, and is not

    
    
      
  • The Forth and the kernel are the board's own code, compiled unchanged: the console
        and the pool of slots (src/), the hard kernel of Escapement under EDF
        (EscapementHard.c) with the four tasks of the Pico, one a period. Only the
        layer under them differs (web/platform_web.c, web/kernel/).
    • 
      
  • The console is the kernel's idle task here: the slots preempt it, and one another,
        wherever the Forth reads the clock, every sixteen words. On the board the console has
        a core of its own, and never delays a slot.
    • 
      
  • The kernel's clock follows the browser's, which lets the page run every few
        milliseconds, seldom in a tab left in the background. The time it held the page, the
        clock passes release by release, each on time for the kernel, late on the wall; past
        200 ms, the rest is not given at all. A pause of the browser of more than half a
        millisecond or so in the middle of a run, a garbage collection or the system, is left
        out of the clock and of the times measured: the board has none.
    • 
      
  • The times the pool, the timeline and slots show are those of your
        browser, not of the Pico, read to a tenth of a millisecond, or a whole one, depending on
        the browser, coarser still where it guards your privacy, the page then saying so: a
        short word reads 0, and an empty task takes no width. Nothing here tells
        the costs of the kernel on a chip, nor its energy.
    • 
      
  • A line typed at the console is stopped after 10 s, as Ctrl-C does on the board. The
        dictionary lives as long as the page; save has no flash to write.
